> [!abstract]
> A clone of [[Malik Monk]], derived to revolve entirely around **Patience** — the absolute refusal to act before the correct moment.

## Virtue — Patience
Malik reads Wrath as the sin of premature action: responding to the world before God's timing rather than waiting for the right moment. Patience is the discipline to hold — to absorb, endure, and wait — until the moment chosen by a higher order arrives.

- **Malik's interpretation**: Anger is proof that someone believes their own timeline matters more than God's. Patience is the acknowledgement that the correct outcome is inevitable — it only requires waiting.

## Personality
Patience is the most unsettling of the seven to be around. She speaks slowly, chooses every word, and does not fill silences — she lets them run as long as they need to. She will sit across from someone in complete stillness for an hour without giving any indication she is uncomfortable. Most people cannot do the same.

She is not passive or withdrawn. She is fully present and paying close attention. She simply does not act until she has identified the exact right moment to do so, and she has infinite tolerance for how long that takes. She cannot be provoked. Insults, threats, and frustration tactics don't register as useful inputs to her — she files them away as information about the person delivering them.

- Eerily calm in all circumstances; her composure never cracks.
- Speaks last in any group conversation, always.
- Never interrupts. Will wait mid-sentence for someone to finish a thought they've trailed off from.
- Her stillness makes others hyperaware of their own fidgeting and impatience.

## Combat Role
Counter-fighter and opportunist. Patience does not create openings — she waits for the opponent to create one, then acts with absolute precision. Her responses are calibrated to be exactly one step ahead of whatever the opponent just committed to. She is nearly impossible to bait.

## Relationships
- [[Malik Monk]] — creator; this clone embodies the long-game thinking at the core of Malik's plan.
